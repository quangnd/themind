# Vienna School of Agentic Coding

A name given by spectators — not self-applied — to the loose technical friendship and collaboration between [[Mario Zechner]], [[Armin Ronacher]], and Peter Steinberger. The three Austrian/Vienna-connected software engineers converged on agentic coding in 2025 and began publicly working through its implications together, influencing each other's tools, ideas, and blog posts.

Mario: *"I'm not Viennese, but I'll allow it."*

## Origin

In April 2025, Peter Steinberger began loudly evangelizing on Twitter: **THE AGENTS, THEY WORK**. Mario and Armin were skeptical, got Claude Code subscriptions, tried them, and "stopped sleeping for a while."

In May 2025, the three of them met at Peter's Vienna flat and built their first joint "vibe slopped" project: **VibeTunnel**. From there: experimenting, commenting on each other's technical blog posts, revising ideas, online and offline.

In September 2025, Peter organized **Claude Code Anonymous Vienna** — a meetup of like-minded agentic coders. That's when Colin (Earendil co-founder) first met Mario, apparently as a soft vibe check.

## Shared Philosophy

What unites them is less a formal doctrine and more a shared intuition: **LLMs are good at writing and running code, so lean into this.** Concretely:

- Minimal agent cores over bloated frameworks
- Agents that build and extend themselves rather than downloading plugins
- Code that runs code as the primary interface
- Skepticism about complexity for its own sake

This is expressed in Armin's [[armin-ronacher-pi-minimal-agent|Pi blog post]], Mario's [[mario-zechner-slowing-the-fuck-down|Slowing the F*** Down essay]], and Pi's design itself.

## Significance

The Vienna School is not an institution or a movement in any formal sense. Its significance is that three experienced, pragmatic engineers converged on similar conclusions about agentic coding from independent directions, then cross-pollinated ideas in public. The output — Pi, OpenClaw, and the writing around them — has had outsized influence on how people think about minimal agent design.

The formation of [[Earendil]] is the institutional crystallization of some of these ideas.
