# Fast Content vs Slow Content

A framework from [[Dan Koe]] that distinguishes content by what it does to the consumer's capacity for thought — not by length or format, but by whether it *requires* thinking.

## Fast Content (Entropic)

Fast content delivers conclusions without requiring thought. It's "fast food for the mind" — social media companies use the same psychological triggers as fast food companies who discovered that fat, sugar, and salt exploited scarcity-wiring in the brain.

Examples:
- BuzzFeed listicles
- Rage tweets and hot takes
- AI-generated summaries
- Engagement-optimized threads
- TikTok explainers that give the *feeling* of understanding in 30 seconds

Fast content skips the ordering process and delivers pre-packaged conclusions. The consumer's consciousness stays disordered — they feel informed but empty. This is what Koe calls **psychic entropy**: attention fragmented, scattered, pulled in competing directions. It manifests as anxiety, boredom, and restlessness.

> The early adopters of the internet could post "Top 10 habits to get rich by age 30" and be swarmed by hundreds of thousands of readers, but the landscape has changed.

## Slow Content (Centropic)

Slow content requires both creator and consumer to engage in genuine cognitive effort. It demands that you think to receive the insight.

Examples:
- Essays
- Long-form non-optimized conversations
- Certain books and lectures
- Even tweets crafted to make you *think* rather than react

Slow content engages the ordering process. The writer orders their own consciousness through the act of creation. The reader re-orders their thoughts by properly digesting the thinking. This is **psychic negentropy**: attention invested in a complex, challenging activity with clear feedback. It feels like flow, purpose, and [[The Meaning Economy|meaning]].

## The Connection to Meaning

The distinction maps directly onto the concept of [[The Meaning Economy|ordered vs disordered consciousness]]:

1. **Fast content** → psychic entropy → feels informed but empty
2. **Slow content** → psychic negentropy → generates genuine meaning

This is why someone can spend hours scrolling and feel drained, while spending the same hours writing or reading an essay feels energizing. The difference isn't time spent — it's whether consciousness was *ordered* in the process.

## Why This Matters Now

AI has supercharged the fast content end of the spectrum. It can produce content that *mimics* real thinking without requiring cognitive effort from either creator or consumer. The [[Epistemic Commons]] becomes flooded with imitation thinking at scale.

Koe's argument is that the future belongs to slow content — particularly [[Essays as Thinking Tools|essays]] — because they are the one form that AI structurally cannot replicate. An essay requires a situated point of view, direct experience, beliefs that shift through the writing process, and the capacity for genuine surprise and discovery.

## The Input Metabolism Problem

A related concept from Koe: consuming information without processing time is like eating without digestion. The mind, like the digestive system, has a metabolic limit. Consuming 10 podcasts a week without gaps doesn't produce knowledge — it produces bloat. The problem isn't bad inputs per se; it's the ratio of input to processing time.

This connects directly to the [[Default Mode Network]]: integration and synthesis can only happen in the gaps between consumption. A life saturated with fast content has no activation windows for the insight mechanism to run.
