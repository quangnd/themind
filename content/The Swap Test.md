# The Swap Test

A simple heuristic from [[Dan Koe]] for determining whether AI can replace a given piece of creative work:

> **If you could swap the creator and the creation would be just as valuable, then AI can replace it. If the creation only works because *you* made it — because it carries your perspective, your situation, your taste — that is your edge.**

---

## What It Identifies

Some work has an **objective floor**: it needs to be accurate, clear, and complete, and any competent producer can deliver that. Documentation, summaries, instructions, formatted reports — the creator is irrelevant to the value of the output. Swap in AI, swap in a different human, the result is equally useful. AI should do this work.

Other work is **author-dependent**: the value is not separable from the identity, trajectory, and situated perspective of the person who made it. A personal essay about grief after losing a parent. An argument for why minimalism is a trap for creatives written from years of lived contradiction. The creator cannot be swapped without destroying the thing.

---

## What AI Cannot Access

Koe enumerates what makes a human creative truly irreplaceable:

- **Perspective in the first person** — AI can think *about* your perspective or *in the style of* your perspective, but it cannot think *from* it. It lacks the specific accumulation of beliefs, experiences, and contradictions that lead you to notice a particular thing and care about it in a particular way.
- **Energy signature** — the selection of what to focus on, and why it matters *right now*. This is a function of where you are in your life at this moment, not a simulatable property.
- **Genuine sensemaking** — deciding what information means, what's important, how to frame it. AI can process and combine; it cannot decide what matters from a lived position.
- **Trajectory and stakes** — AI has no death. It has no arc. It does not have the natural compression of time that makes human stories carry weight. Even if mortality becomes a solved problem, each human moment passes permanently. AI simulates stories; humans live them.
- **Evolving taste** — AI does not look back on its output from a year ago and disagree with it. It does not grow. A creative who publishes consistently is always one step ahead: by the time AI has learned to replicate where you were, you have already moved on. What is "great work" is a moving target; AI is always chasing a prior position.

---

## The Practical Implication

The Swap Test doesn't say "never use AI." It draws a line. Use AI for anything that passes the swap — where you are interchangeable with the output. Do not hand AI the work that only you can do, because that is precisely your competitive position.

The distinction maps onto the difference between **content** (replicable information) and **perspective** (irreplaceable situated intelligence). As AI commoditizes the former, the latter appreciates in value.

This is the economic core of [[The Meaning Economy]]: when anyone can produce content instantly, content becomes worthless. The scarce good becomes the person behind it — their story, their struggle, their specific way of seeing.

---

## The Slop Counterargument

A common objection: AI is flooding the internet with slop, making it impossible to be seen. Koe's response: this is actually good news. When AI can produce infinite volume instantly, volume becomes worthless. Differentiation becomes easier, not harder. "Once everyone can do it instantly, it becomes instantly worthless." The only thing that cannot be commoditized is a perspective that is genuinely yours.
