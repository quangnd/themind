# Techno-Economic Evolution and Worldviews

A framework for understanding how societies develop — not just economically, but in terms of the meaning-making systems and values that dominate each era. The key insight: **the dominant technology of a society creates the conditions for a new worldview to become possible at scale.** The two tracks evolve together, each enabling the next stage of the other.

Drawn from [[Dan Koe]]'s synthesis of developmental theory, integral theory, and economic history.

---

## The Two Tracks

### Track 1: Techno-Economic Base

The dominant mode of production and primary technologies of each era:

| Era | Mode of Production | Primary Technologies |
|-----|-------------------|---------------------|
| Foraging | Hunter-gatherer | Axes, spears, fire |
| Horticultural | Small-plot cultivation | Hoe, digging sticks |
| Agrarian | Large-scale farming | Animal-drawn plow |
| Industrial | Mechanized production | Steam, coal, oil, machinery |
| Informational | Knowledge work | Computers, networks, software |
| Intelligence (emerging) | AI-automated production | Large language models, robotics |

Each transition freed up human labor and time, creating conditions for new classes of people to emerge — the surplus from agrarian farming enabled explorers, merchants, and conquerors; the industrial surplus enabled knowledge workers and professionals; the informational surplus enabled creators and independent operators.

### Track 2: Dominant Worldview

The value system and meaning-making structure of people within each era:

| Worldview | Era | Meaning Source | Key Value |
|-----------|-----|----------------|-----------|
| Premodern | Foraging → Agrarian | Given by God/elders/tradition | Conformity and divine authority |
| Modern | Industrial | Discovered through reason and science | Rationality, progress, merit |
| Postmodern | Informational | Deconstructed — no single truth | Relativity, inclusion, aperspectivalism |
| Metamodern (emerging) | Intelligence | Generated from within | Agency, taste, self-authorship |

## The Arc of Meaning

One of the most useful things this framework surfaces is how the *source* of meaning has shifted:

- **Premodern → "Up There"** — Meaning is given by a higher power. Gods, elders, kings, priests. You are told what to believe; conformity is the price of belonging. Agency is dangerous.
- **Modern → "Out There"** — Meaning is earned through productivity and progress. Science replaces the divine. Nature is disenchanted. Merit replaces birthright. Work becomes the organizing religion.
- **Postmodern → "Nowhere"** — Meaning is deconstructed. All perspectives are equally valid; no hierarchy of truth is allowed. This is an important achievement (no one view is absolute) but it collapses into a contradiction: claiming all perspectives are equal is itself a hierarchical value claim.
- **Metamodern → "In Here"** — Meaning must be self-generated. Not found, not earned from a job, not rejected as impossible — but actively created through the process of ordering consciousness, pursuing struggle, and contributing to something beyond yourself.

> "Postmodernism correctly saw that no perspective is absolute, but then incorrectly concluded that no perspective is better. Some perspectives are, in fact, better."

This matters because the postmodern refusal to rank perspectives blocks development. Some worldviews genuinely metabolize more complexity, hold more contradiction, and produce better outcomes. Acknowledging this is not a return to dogmatism — it is the precondition for moving forward.

## The Meaning Crisis as a Stage Transition

The [[Metacrisis]] and the broader meaning crisis are, in this framework, symptoms of a failed transition. The industrial worldview gave people identity through productivity and progress. The postmodern deconstruction removed that without offering a replacement. Result: people stripped of the God narrative and the productivity narrative, with no self-generated alternative. Meaninglessness, nihilism, and the sense that nothing matters.

The informational age (social media, algorithmic content, AI) has accelerated this crisis rather than solving it. Content volume exploded while meaning density collapsed — the [[Epistemic Commons]] flooded with attention-maximizing noise.

The next stage — the intelligence age — creates the material conditions for the metamodern worldview to become widespread: when AI handles the necessities, meaning must come from the inside. This is both the threat and the opportunity.

---

## Why This Matters for Creatives

Koe draws several practical implications from this framework:

1. **Taste becomes essential** — In a postmodern worldview where nothing is better, you can't develop genuine taste (which requires saying some things are better). The metamodern must reclaim this.
2. **Agency becomes survival** — The premodern and modern worldviews supplied identity from outside (God, employer). In the intelligence age, no institution will supply it; it must be actively authored.
3. **Individual perspective may be the competitive moat** — If no perspective is privileged by default, the one who has done the developmental work to hold the most complex, least dogmatic, most genuinely original perspective is the scarce good. See [[The Swap Test]].
4. **Creatives are meaning-architects** — If meaning must be generated from within and people don't know how to do that, the people who do will be in enormous demand. See [[The Post-Labor Skill Stack]].
