# George Orwell

**George Orwell** (1903–1950), born Eric Arthur Blair, was an English novelist, essayist, and critic. Best known for *Animal Farm* (1945) and *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (1949), his work is defined by a commitment to plain language, political honesty, and opposition to totalitarianism.

## Why He Matters for This Wiki

Orwell's [[Politics and the English Language]] (1946) is a foundational text for the Writing Craft cluster. His central argument — that **unclear language is not just bad style but bad thinking**, and that political manipulation depends on linguistic fog — connects directly to:

- [[Write Like You Talk]] — Graham's conversational writing method is a modern application of Orwell's sixth rule ("Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous")
- [[Putting Ideas into Words]] — Graham's argument that writing forces thinking echoes Orwell's vicious cycle thesis
- [[Clarity is Overrated]] — Gioia's pro-ambiguity stance creates a productive tension: Orwell fights euphemism and vagueness; Gioia fights sterility and over-simplification. They have **different enemies**
- [[Epistemic Commons]] — Orwell diagnosed the same poisoning of shared discourse, but in an era of state propaganda rather than algorithmic feeds
- [[Collapse of the Knowledge System]] — language decay as an early symptom of institutional knowledge failure

## Key Works

- *Politics and the English Language* (1946) — essay on language decay and political euphemism
- *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (1949) — dystopian novel; Newspeak as the ultimate application of his language thesis
- *Animal Farm* (1945) — political allegory
- "Why I Write" (1946) — autobiographical essay on his motivations
- *Homage to Catalonia* (1938) — firsthand account of the Spanish Civil War
- *Down and Out in Paris and London* (1933) — memoir of poverty
