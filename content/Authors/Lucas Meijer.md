# Lucas Meijer

Dutch software engineer and game engine developer. Co-creator of **Unity** (the game engine). Now building a new AI-oriented game engine designed for humans and agents working together.

Runs a bi-monthly "coping session" for brownfield programmers adapting to AI agents. Strong Pi advocate. Prefers **Codex** for writing software and **Pi** for its hackability and context control.

## Views on AI Agents

Lucas is pragmatic and skeptical of "stage 9" AI maximalism (agent swarms, fully autonomous multi-agent systems). His focus is on what works for one developer working with a handful of parallel workstreams, each waiting for human evaluation.

Key positions:
- **Don't anthropomorphize** — an agent is a token-producing machine, not a human to converse with
- **Evaluation is the bottleneck** — in any parallel agent workflow, the human evaluator is the constraint; optimize there first
- **Experiment over prescription** — we're too early to know what ergonomic AI assistance looks like; hackable tools enable the experimentation needed
- **Solve actual problems** — build extensions for friction you actually have, not hypothetical scale problems

On the stage 9 / swarm camp: *"I don't know what those guys are smoking. I'm not having any of that."*

Honest about the collective uncertainty: *"None of us know what we're doing with coding agents. We have no idea what ergonomic AI assistance looks like. We're so early."*

## "Barba Papa Software"

A concept Lucas introduced: software that shapeshifts into whatever form is needed for the user and the task at hand — rather than one monolith written once for thousands of users. Named after the 1970s French/Dutch cartoon character Barba Papa, who shapeshifts on adventures.

Pi embodies this: it has access to its own docs and can write extensions for itself via `/reload` hot reload. Lucas sees this as a model for his new game engine as well.
