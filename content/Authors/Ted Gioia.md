# Ted Gioia

Ted Gioia is a music historian, jazz critic, and writer best known for his Substack newsletter *The Honest Broker*. What makes him an unusual figure is the gap between his formal credentials — business degrees, no music training — and his depth of knowledge across literature, philosophy, history, and music, all built through a decades-long [[Ted Gioia's Reading Methodology|self-directed reading program]].

## Background

- Grew up working class in an Italian-Mexican household; parents never went to college
- Encountered "smug responses" to his efforts to read great books as a young man — told this kind of education wasn't relevant for someone like him; he considered this patronizing in the extreme
- Tutored Stanford freshmen in the university's great books program as an undergraduate, watching firsthand how life-changing that immersion could be
- Earned degrees from elite institutions but considers more than 90% of his education self-directed
- Made his name as a jazz writer and music historian without ever taking a formal music class
- Didn't begin writing about literary subjects until his forties — by design, not accident
- Known for having an unusual aura of *gravitas* that others noticed before he did, which he attributes entirely to his reading
- His older brother Dana — seven years his senior — was his primary early guide, pointing him toward great books, music, visual arts, and films from childhood; Gioia calls him "one of the most well-read and thoughtful people on the planet"

## Core Philosophy

Gioia's worldview is built on a single teenage conviction: that wisdom is real, worth pursuing, and accessible to anyone willing to do the work. This sounds naïve — he knows it does — but he treats it as a foundation, not a naivety.

His reading is deliberately **holistic, not vocational**. Books for inner life, kept entirely separate from career. He didn't put work-related books on his lifetime reading list. The separation was intentional: he wanted a reading life that served the soul, not the résumé.

The counterintuitive result: the inner life eventually transformed the career anyway, in ways he never planned or sought.

## Deep Dive Methodology

Beyond the lifetime reading plan, Gioia practices a separate habit: picking a specific topic and devoting several months to intense immersion — typically ~12 books on the subject. Recent deep dives:

- Origins of Romanticism
- Shakespeare's life and times
- The decline of the Roman Empire
- Next planned: mysticism as a force in intellectual history

He also pursues lighter side projects — e.g., a current read-through of fiction about chess (*Stefan Zweig's chess novel*, then *Nabokov's The Luzhin Defense*). The deep dive pattern is distinct from the lifetime reading plan's breadth-first curriculum approach: it goes narrow and vertical rather than broad and horizontal.

## Resonances

Gioia's project connects interestingly to [[Essays as Thinking Tools]] — both argue that slow, effortful engagement with ideas builds something that fast consumption cannot. His lifetime reading plan is, in a sense, the reader's equivalent of essay writing: an act of genuine cognitive ordering rather than passive information intake.
