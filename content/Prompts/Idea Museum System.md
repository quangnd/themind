# Idea Museum System

A content creation system from [[Dan Koe]] for building a reliable, compounding source of high-signal ideas. The system has three phases: building the museum, curating for density, and developing range by writing one idea many ways.

The goal: a library of ideas so good that people can't help but engage — and that you would never think to ask AI for, because they're yours.

---

## Phase 1 — Build the Museum

An idea museum is an ongoing personal repository of high-signal ideas, quotes, observations, and sources. Format doesn't matter — Apple Notes, Notion, a physical notebook. The habit matters more than the structure.

**The single critical habit:** When you encounter an idea that feels useful, write it down immediately. Don't wait. Don't curate later. Capture the impulse before it fades.

Two signals to watch for when deciding what's worth capturing:
- **Performance signal** — would this resonate with others? Gauge by how similar ideas perform for accounts you respect
- **Excitement signal** — does this feel wasteful *not* to write down? That feeling is the signal

### Your 3–5 Primary Sources

Choose sources with high idea density — where the signal-to-noise ratio is exceptional. Aim for at least one from each category:

1. **Old or little-known books** — timeless principles untouched by trends; re-read the same 3–5 great ones rather than racing through new ones. [[Ted Gioia's Reading Methodology|Gioia's slow reading approach]] applies here.
2. **Curated compilations** — blogs (Farnam Street), annotated accounts (Navalism), themed anthologies. Someone else has already done the first-pass filtering.
3. **High-signal social accounts** — a personal list of 5 accounts that consistently post ideas worth reacting to. When you have nothing to write, scroll these and find something you have an opinion on.

Finding your sources takes a few months of discovery. Quality > quantity. Five great sources beats fifty average ones.

## Phase 2 — Curate for Idea Density

Idea density is how much signal is packed into a piece of content. It's subjective — it depends on your development level and your audience's. What's obvious to you can be life-changing for someone earlier on the same path.

To increase your idea density over time:

1. **Remove low-signal sources.** If a source rarely produces something worth capturing, cut it.
2. **Tag what performs.** When an idea you captured later produces strong content, note what made it high-signal. Pattern-match over time.
3. **Look for the non-obvious connection.** The highest-density ideas are usually intersections — two concepts from different domains, combined in a way that makes both clearer. This is the [[Generalists vs Specialists|generalist advantage]] at the content level.

---

## Phase 3 — Write 1 Idea 1000 Ways

Content quality is not just the idea. It's how the idea is structured, sequenced, and framed. The same insight can produce radically different impact depending on form:

- **Observation format:** *"One pattern I've noticed in happy people: they're obsessive about maintaining their mental clarity."*
- **List format:** *"Happy people are clear-minded people: they take time for rest, focus on one goal, ruthlessly eliminate distractions."*
- **Contrast:** *"Most people think happiness is found. Clear-minded people know it's built."*
- **Question:** *"What if the reason you feel foggy isn't stress — it's that you've never protected your clarity the way happy people do?"*

Same idea. Different structures. Different audiences reached.

### Practice Protocol

**Step 1 — Deconstruct 3 posts**
Pick 3 ideas from your museum that resonated with you. Break each one down: what is the core claim, how are the sentences structured, what psychological mechanism makes it engaging? Use this AI prompt if needed:

> Do a comprehensive analysis of this post. Break down the overall idea, sentence structure, and word choice. Analyze why people engage with it, what psychological tactics are at work, and give me step-by-step instructions for replicating this style with my own ideas.

Paste the post below. (Koe recommends Claude over other models for this.)

**Step 2 — Rewrite 1 idea with 3 structures**
Choose one idea from your museum. Rewrite it using the three structures you just deconstructed. This is how you develop range, stop staring at blank screens, and turn one idea into a week of content.

---

## The Compounding Effect

The idea museum does not plateau — it compounds. Each new idea connects to prior ones. Your writing develops a coherent throughline. Over months, your brand becomes a library of ideas so dense and interconnected that it cannot be replicated by someone without your specific background and trajectory.

This is the moat. Not follower count. Not posting frequency. The accumulated specificity of the mind you are building.
