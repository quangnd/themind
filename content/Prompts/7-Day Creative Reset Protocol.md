# 7-Day Creative Reset Protocol

A staged protocol for recovering creative state from cognitive burnout. Developed by [[Dan Koe]]. The underlying mechanisms: [[Default Mode Network]] activation, [[Hedonic Adaptation|dopamine receptor resensitization]], and [[Reticular Activating System|RAS reprogramming]] through a meaningful project.

The protocol is simple. That will cause people to dismiss it. The difficulty is behavioral, not intellectual.

---

## Days 1–2: Reduce The Input Fast

*Equivalent to intermittent fasting for the mind. Create space.*

- **Strict work timeblocks.** Limit work to 4 hours if possible. Set an alarm that marks the end — when it goes off, you're done. No "one last task." Practice letting something feel unfinished without anxiety.
- **Cut your primary mindless input source.** The podcast on the commute, the scroll before bed, the news in the morning — identify the one you reach for most automatically. Replace it with nothing. Sit in silence. Listen for an idea.
- **Go on a walk.** No headphones. Leave the phone at home if you can. Don't expect much from this first walk — the point is to start the habit and let the [[Default Mode Network]] begin activating.

**Why it works:** Removing constant input allows the brain's default mode network to fire. It cannot be active while consuming.

---

## Days 3–4: Digest What's Already There

*Space has been created. Things will surface. Let them.*

Maintain the habits from Days 1–2, plus:

- **Read one chapter of a book slowly.** Not to finish it, not to extract value — just to notice when a sentence makes you stop and think. When it does, put the book down. Sit with why that line hit you. One idea fully absorbed beats an entire book skimmed.
- **Sit with nothing for 10 minutes.** No app, no guided breathing, no technique. Just sit somewhere and let your mind do whatever it wants. It will be chaotic. That's the digestion happening. Don't do anything with it.
- **Continue the walk. Actually see things.** You've passed by these things a hundred times. Notice the detail in the sidewalk, the structure of the tree branches, the sky. Did you even look up on the last walk?

**Why it works:** Releasing grip on the subconscious — stepping away from task-oriented work — is what allows suppressed connections and ideas to surface. Stepping away from work to let the DMN process is often more productive than working.

---

## Days 5–6: Become Interested in Life Again

*The fog should be lifting. Colors more vivid, conversations more interesting, small things meaningful again.*

Maintain all previous habits, plus:

- **Trust that ideas will come back.** Resist the urge to immediately capture everything. If it's important, it will find its way back. When an interesting thought appears, let it play out — stay in that stream of consciousness. Don't reach for your phone.
- **Have one real conversation.** Not catching up, not networking. No performing. Listen to another person's perspective and try to be genuinely present. Your brain may light up.
- **Extend the walk.** You might find you don't want to stop.

**Why it works:** [[Hedonic Adaptation|Dopamine receptors are resensitizing]]. You begin doing things because they're inherently interesting — intrinsic motivation returning. This is the precondition for genuine creative output.

---

## Day 7: Create With What's Emerged

*Create from abundance, not obligation.*

- **Make something with no plan.** Write, draw, record a voice note, cook without a recipe. The only rule: no rules. No strategic thinking, no searching for the perfect angle. Start a thread and follow it.
- **Don't share it.** For at least 24 hours. Make something that's entirely yours, without the silent influence of imagined audience. Notice how that feels.
- After 24 hours, return to it. If you want to share it, do. If not, that's fine too.

**Why it works:** Separating **generative thinking** (producing novel ideas) from **evaluative thinking** (judging and editing) is essential. Trying to do both simultaneously suppresses the generative phase. Day 7 is pure generation.

---

## After the Protocol

The reset clears the interference. The next step is identifying the [[Reticular Activating System|meaningful project]] that will reprogram the RAS and make creativity consistent rather than accidental.

Without a project, your mind is a boat on a calm sea with no destination. The reset is necessary but not sufficient.
