# Life Launch Protocol

A one-day psychological excavation protocol by [[Dan Koe]] for uncovering what you actually want from life, breaking unconscious patterns, and entering a season of intense progress. Built on the [[Identity-First Change]] insight that real change requires changing who you are, not just what you do.

Reserve a full day. Do not outsource any of this to AI — the point is to break past the limiter on your own mind.

---

## Part 1 — Morning: Psychological Excavation

*Set aside 15–30 minutes. Work through these questions in writing. If you can't answer immediately, leave space and return.*

### Section A — Surfacing the Current Reality

These questions surface the pain in your current life. Answer honestly, not charitably.

1. What is the dull, persistent dissatisfaction you've learned to live with? Not deep suffering — what you've simply tolerated. (If you don't hate it, you'll keep tolerating it.)
2. What do you complain about repeatedly but never actually change? List the three complaints you've voiced most in the past year.
3. For each complaint: what would someone who observed your *behavior* (not your words) conclude you actually want?
4. What truth about your current life would be unbearable to admit to someone you deeply respect?

### Section B — The Anti-Vision

A brutally specific picture of the life you do not want. Use it as negative fuel — not to dwell in fear, but to make inaction's cost undeniable.

1. If absolutely nothing changes for five years, describe an average Tuesday. Where do you wake up? What does your body feel like? What's the first thing you think about? Who's around you? What do you do from 9am to 6pm? How do you feel at 10pm?
2. Do the same for ten years. What have you missed? What opportunities closed? Who gave up on you? What do people say about you when you're not in the room?
3. You're at the end of your life. You lived the safe version. You never broke the pattern. What was the cost? What did you never let yourself feel, try, or become?
4. Who in your life is already living the future you just described — someone five, ten, twenty years ahead on the same trajectory? What do you feel when you picture becoming them?
5. What identity would you have to give up to actually change? ("I am the type of person who...") What would it cost you socially to no longer be that person?
6. What is the most embarrassing reason you haven't changed — the one that makes you sound weak, scared, or lazy rather than reasonable?
7. If your current behavior is a form of self-protection, what are you protecting? And what is that protection costing you?

### Section C — The Minimum Viable Vision

Orient the released energy in a positive direction. Start rough — a vision is a product; it grows more precise over time.

1. Forget practicality. If you could snap your fingers and be living a different life in three years — not what's realistic, what you *actually want* — describe an average Tuesday. Same level of detail as above.
2. What would you have to believe about yourself for that life to feel natural rather than forced? Write the identity statement: "I am the type of person who..."
3. What is one thing you would do this week if you were already that person?

## Part 2 — Throughout the Day: Interrupting Autopilot

*Set these as phone reminders with the question included — not just an alarm. Schedule them during commuting, walking, or idle time.*

- **11:00am** — What am I avoiding right now by doing what I'm doing?
- **1:30pm** — If someone filmed the last two hours, what would they conclude I want from my life?
- **3:15pm** — Am I moving toward the life I hate or the life I want?
- **5:00pm** — What's the most important thing I'm pretending isn't important?
- **7:30pm** — What did I do today out of identity protection rather than genuine desire?
- **9:00pm** — When did I feel most alive today? When did I feel most dead?

Additional contemplation prompts for walks or downtime:
- What would change if I stopped needing people to see me as [the identity from question 5]?
- Where in my life am I trading aliveness for safety?
- What's the smallest version of the person I want to become that I could be tomorrow?

---

## Part 3 — Evening: Synthesis

*Integrate the day's insights into a direction you will act on tomorrow.*

1. After today, what feels most true about why you've been stuck?
2. What is the actual enemy? Not circumstances, not other people — the internal pattern or belief that has been running the show. Name it clearly.
3. Write one sentence that captures what you refuse to let your life become. (Your anti-vision compressed. It should make you feel something.)
4. Write one sentence that captures what you're building toward, knowing it will evolve. (Your vision MVP.)

### Setting the Goals (as Lenses, not Targets)

These are not productivity goals. They are points of view — lenses that put you in the right state of mind to take the actions that lead away from the life you don't want.

1. **One-year lens** — What would have to be true in one year for you to know you've broken the old pattern? One concrete thing.
2. **One-month lens** — What would have to be true in one month for the one-year lens to remain possible?
3. **Daily lens** — What are 2–3 actions you can time-block tomorrow that the person you're becoming would simply do?
