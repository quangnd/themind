# Essays as Thinking Tools

[[Dan Koe]] argues that essays are "the last bastion of real thinking" — the one form of content that AI structurally cannot replicate, and the most powerful tool for developing your capacity to make sense of reality.

## Essays vs Articles

A crucial distinction that clarifies what makes essays unique:

|           | Articles                         | Essays                      |
| --------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Purpose   | Answers                          | Arguments                   |
| Knowledge | Package existing knowledge       | Change the author's beliefs |
| Structure | Start with the conclusion        | Figure it out along the way |
| Function  | Inform or educate                | An act of thinking          |
| Discovery | Communicate what's already there | Discover what isn't         |

The defining characteristic: an essay is a process of *discovery*. ==The writer doesn't know the conclusion when they begin==. The act of writing *is* the thinking.

## Why AI Cannot Write Essays

This is the core of Koe's argument and it's worth sitting with:

1. **No situated point of view.** AI can simulate any perspective you tell it to adopt, but it lacks the beliefs, biases, emotions, and direct experience that lead a human to think and question in a particular direction.
2. **No genuine discovery.** You can ask AI to share something novel, but then you're *anticipating* it. The surprise is destroyed. The magic of stumbling upon an unexpected insight — the moment that reshapes your thinking — requires not knowing what you're looking for.
3. **No evolving context.** Every passing moment influences a human's point of view. To have a meaningful perspective, you must engage in novel experiences, not sit at a desk conversing with Claude all day.
4. **Creativity exhaustion.** Koe notes that while AI is useful, it "exhausts creativity extremely fast." ==The more you outsource thinking, the less thinking capacity you build==.

This doesn't mean AI is useless for writing — it can help with research, structure, and editing. But the *thinking* must come from the human, or you've created an article, not an essay.

## How to Start Writing Essays

Koe's practical advice:

- **Write to discover, not to perform.** Begin with uncertainty and an open mind — a concept, question, experience, or something that bothers you.
- **Write about what genuinely interests you.** Focus on a single main idea. Research deeply. Challenge every point of view. Don't accept one source as law.
- **Resist the template.** Just write. Have a debate with yourself. Ask questions to keep going. Worry about structure later.
- **Ask, "Do I actually believe this?"** The point is to *change* what you believe, not confirm it. Resist the urge to be absolutely right.
- **Read essays, consume centropic content.** Your sense-making capacity is shaped by your inputs. Actively search and curate — don't rely on the algorithm.
- **Build a body of work, not a content calendar.** Each essay builds on the last. AI can't replicate a coherent philosophy built through years of genuine thinking.

## Notable Essayists Referenced

Koe points to minds forged through essay writing: Paul Graham, Isaac Newton, Jordan Peterson (in his prime), Nietzsche, Emerson.

## The Deeper Connection

Essay writing is not just a content strategy — it's a mechanism for ordering consciousness. Through the struggle of articulating an idea, the writer creates [[The Meaning Economy|meaning]] for themselves and potentially thousands of readers across decades. This makes essays the most scalable and durable form of [[Fast Content vs Slow Content|slow content]].

A meaningful conversation can shift one person but lives and dies in memory. An essay develops thinking capacity and can do that for thousands across decades.
