# The Meaning Economy

The meaning economy is an emerging paradigm where meaning — not information, not productivity, not material goods — becomes the scarcest and most valuable commodity. [[Dan Koe]] argues this shift is already underway and AI has only accelerated it.

## The Arc of Scarcity

Each era has organized itself around what was scarce:

| Era | God / Organizing Principle | What Was Scarce |
|-----|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Pre-industrial | Gods in the sky | Explanation of the world |
| Industrial | Productivity | Labor and output |
| Post-industrial (now) | More — more money, more information, more content | **Meaning and purpose** |

==We have more stuff and less purpose than ever.== The logical conclusion: meaning will be sold at a premium, and people will crave it more than they already do.

## What Meaning Actually Is

Meaning is not something you *find* in the world. It's the **experience of ordered consciousness** — a state that emerges when attention is invested in something complex enough to fully engage you.

### Psychic Entropy (Disorder)
When attention is fragmented, scattered, and pulled in competing directions:
- Feels like: anxiety, boredom, restlessness
- Caused by: [[Fast Content vs Slow Content|fast content]], endless scrolling, reactive consumption
- Result: chaos, no meaning generated

### Psychic Negentropy (Order)
When attention is invested in a complex, challenging activity with clear feedback:
- Feels like: flow, purpose, meaning
- Caused by: [[Fast Content vs Slow Content|slow content]], essay writing, deep work
- Result: ordered consciousness, meaning created

The key insight: meaning is **created through the process of ordering consciousness itself**. Taking chaos and creating structure. Wrestling with complexity until it coheres. This is why writing an essay — struggling through drafts, synthesizing a point of view — produces meaning for the writer, and reading one can produce meaning for the reader.

> If you are level 10, a level 1 challenge is boring and a level 20 challenge is daunting. But a level 11 challenge is just enough to lock you in.

This echoes Csikszentmihalyi's concept of flow — the zone between boredom and anxiety where challenge matches skill.

## The Two Pillars of Meaning

Meaning, at the individual level, is built on two structural requirements:

1. **Progress** — the feeling of forward movement, growth, becoming. Without it, you don't stay static; you slowly fall into chaos. Entropy applies to the mind as well as physical systems.
2. **Contribution** — connection to something greater than yourself. Isolation accelerates meaninglessness; belonging to a tribe, a cause, or a shared project is the other pillar.

For most of modern history, both pillars were outsourced: progress was supplied by an employer (career ladder, salary increases, role progression) and contribution was supplied by a religious community or civic institution. In the intelligence age, both must be self-generated.

Progress and contribution are activated through **creative problem-solving**: set an aim, identify the obstacle, create a solution, pass it to others in a similar position. If they find it valuable, they exchange value back. This is the basic unit of the creator economy.

## The Three Generators of Meaning

Within the two pillars, three specific mechanisms generate meaning:

1. **Struggle** — the engine of progress. What you choose to struggle for is your purpose. Struggle requires deliberate choice, rejection of the default path, creation of genuine failure potential, and the development of a character arc. Without struggle there is no story.
2. **Curiosity** — the direction of progress. The non-linear, personally specific choice of where to direct attention. Curiosity is how you filter signal from noise in a way only you could. Distraction erodes it; depth amplifies it.
3. **Status** — the proof of contribution. Recognition from others that your work mattered. This completes the loop: you struggled, created something, and the world acknowledged it as valuable. In the future, status will increasingly come through creative contribution rather than occupational rank.

When these three are active simultaneously, you have a story — and stories, authentic human stories rooted in genuine struggle and real stakes, are what will command a premium in the intelligence age. See [[The Post-Labor Skill Stack]] for the skills that enable this.

## The Meaning Arc

How the *source* of meaning has shifted across [[Techno-Economic Evolution|techno-economic stages]]:

| Stage | Source | Character |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Premodern | "Up There" (God, elders) | Meaning is *given* |
| Modern | "Out There" (productivity, progress) | Meaning is *earned* |
| Postmodern | "Nowhere" (all perspectives equal) | Meaning is *deconstructed* |
| Metamodern (emerging) | "In Here" (self-generated) | Meaning is *created* |

The meaning crisis is the gap between the deconstruction of the old sources and the development of the capacity to generate meaning from within. Most people are stuck in that gap.

## The Opportunity

The world doesn't need more rage-bait or productivity-maximizing AI startups. It needs ordinary people who **make sense of their own minds and document them in public**.

Koe calls these people "value creators" — distinct from typical influencers or personal brands. A value creator:
- Chooses a positive trajectory for their life
- Cares deeply about the interests and skills that move the needle
- Passes their journey down from their point of view to others who relate and benefit

This is the root of the cascade: information influences identity, identity influences behavior, behavior influences the flourishing or destruction of civilization. Shaping the [[Epistemic Commons]] with genuine thinking may be more powerful than building rockets.
