Would you believe me if I told you that the biggest news story of our century is happening right now—but is never mentioned in the press?

The biggest changes often happen long before they even get a name. The word *Renaissance* got introduced two hundred years after the start of the Renaissance. The game was already over.

![[Attachments/gioia-school-of-athens.jpeg]]
*The School of Athens*

We are living through a situation like that right now. A total shift—like the magnetic poles reversing. But it doesn't even have a name—not yet.

Let's call it: **The Collapse of the Knowledge System.**

In this regard, it resembles other massive shifts in Western history—the rebirth of humanistic thinking in the early Renaissance, or the rise of Romanticism in the nineteenth century. In these volatile situations, the whole entrenched hierarchy of truth and authority gets totally reversed.

## THE TEN WARNING SIGNS

### 1. Scientific studies don't replicate.
40% or more of published studies fail to replicate. Even worse, fake studies get cited more often than reliable ones.

### 2. Public distrust of experts has reached an intensity never seen before.
Gioia cited Ortega y Gasset's *The Revolt of the Masses* (1929) a decade ago as uncannily accurate. The only experts who still possess authority are blue collar ones. White collar professional expertise is derided at every turn.

![[Attachments/gioia-nyt-experts.png]]

### 3. The career path for knowledge workers is breaking down.
"Learn to code" once summed up the dominant worldview. Now it sounds like a joke. Art history majors now have an easier time finding jobs than computer engineers. Knowledge workers are being fired everywhere.

![[Attachments/gioia-nyt-ai-jobs.png]]

### 4. Funding for science and tech research is disappearing.
The major sources of funding have dried up. Even huge corporations that fund their own research programs are now investing in AI data centers, not scientists. Meta layoffs, Microsoft cuts, Google eliminations — these are not short-term economic trends.

### 5. Universities have lost their prestige, and have made enemies of their core constituencies.
Tuition costs skyrocketed. Value of degrees plummeted. Huge stultifying bureaucracies disconnected from educational priorities. Vultures are circling.

### 6. Plagiarism is getting exposed at all levels — and authorities take it for granted.
From students to corporations to Harvard's president. A healthy knowledge system requires honesty and accountability. Plagiarism is now everywhere — including embedded in dominant technologies.

### 7. AI is imposed everywhere as the new expert system — but when it hallucinates, authorities take it for granted.
The most alarming fact: the people running the knowledge system complacently accept all the deceit, lies, and hallucinations without ethical qualms or anxiety. The public smells the rot. "Nothing is more damaging than a successful launch of an inferior product. Your short-term success ensures your long-term failure."

### 8. Science and technology are increasingly used to manipulate and exploit, not serve.
Software upgrades users fear. Search engines get worse every year. Social media becomes more manipulative. Surveillance increases, privacy erodes. Smartphone bans spreading in schools and communities.

### 9. Scandals are everywhere in the knowledge economy.
Theranos, Sam Bankman-Fried, collapsing meme coins, COVID. A $1.5 billion AI company funded by Microsoft recently revealed it was 700 workers in India pretending to be chatbots. Scams are now business-as-usual. Nobody is shocked anymore.

### 10. Constant bickering about "fake science" — from all political stances. Nobody talks about "true science."
The term "fake science" barely existed in the 20th century. In those days, science was emblematic of truth. When the knowledge structure collapses, science loses its privileged access to truth.

## WHAT REPLACES THE KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM?

Something will replace it. Gioia hints at it:

> Despite all the manipulations, hallucinations, abuses, and dysfunctional excesses of the digital life… symphonies sound as majestic as ever. Philosophy is more necessary than ever. Paintings are still glorious. Great architecture does not collapse. Nature warms the heart. As do poems and epics and myths.

Four closing points:
1. Science and tech will not disappear — but will face intense backlash beyond anything in the last 200 years
2. Tech leaders think the next stage is the Singularity. In fact, the exact opposite is about to unfold.
3. Tech now desperately needs what can only be provided by the humanities and human values.
4. The new knowledge system will be built on human values. Technology will be forced to serve it — or get locked into a losing battle with the new "softer and gentler" knowledge system.
