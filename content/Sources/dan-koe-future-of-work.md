# The Future of Work, Meaning, and Why You Can't Be Replaced

**Author:** Dan Koe (@thedankoe)
**Source:** The Dan Koe Letter (newsletter)

## Summary

A wide-ranging essay on the future of creative work in an AI-accelerated world. Koe argues that AGI threatens the industrial model of employment but cannot replace what is fundamentally human: evolving perspective, genuine sensemaking, and situated creative work.

Structured in six sections:
1. **The meaning crisis** — historical arc from premodern to postmodern worldviews; meaning went from "Up There" to "Out There" to "Nowhere" and must now come "In Here"
2. **AI acceleration** — Post-Labor Economics (David Shapiro); three ways households earn; jobs that persist (experience economy, meaning makers)
3. **The anatomy of meaning** — two pillars (progress + contribution); three generators (struggle, curiosity, status); stories as the premium product
4. **The creator economy = the meaning economy** — attention as the scarce resource; the Swap Test; AI slop and competitive differentiation
5. **The last defensible moat is you** — what AI cannot access: perspective, energy signature, sensemaking, mortality-driven trajectory, evolving taste
6. **The post-labor skill stack** — agency, taste, perspective (ego development), persuasion, technical know-how

## Key Claims

- Techno-economic development creates the conditions for new worldviews and value systems at scale
- Postmodernism correctly saw that no perspective is absolute but incorrectly concluded no perspective is better — some perspectives *are* more developed
- Meaning has evolved: given by God → earned through productivity → deconstructed → must now be self-generated ("In Here")
- Two pillars of meaning: progress (forward movement) + contribution (connection to something greater)
- Three generators: struggle (engine of progress), curiosity (direction of progress), status (proof of contribution)
- The Swap Test: if the creator could be swapped out and the creation would be equally valuable, AI can replace it
- AI can copy where you were, not where you are now; the creative has always moved on
- The post-labor skill stack in order: agency → taste → perspective → persuasion → technical know-how
