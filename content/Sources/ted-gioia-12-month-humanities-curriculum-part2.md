Today we continue our 12-month immersive program in arts and culture. Below I'm sharing my week-by-week guide for months 4 through 6.

Our approach is not strictly chronological — although it is loosely chronological. I am trying to pace and diversify our efforts in order to optimize the reader experience.

Ground rules same as Part 1: ~250 pages/week, music suggestion, visual arts suggestion each week. Group reading yields extraordinary benefits.

---

## WEEK 14
**Virgil:** *Aeneid* Books 1–2
**Ovid:** *Metamorphoses* Book 1
**Selected Roman poems and aphorisms** (Horace, Catullus, Sulpicia, Martial, Latin proverbs)

Essential to understanding the evolution of epic and narrative. Conveys the national pride, borrowed mythology, and homegrown decadence of Rome. Extracts ~100 pages; supplement with *The Portable Roman Reader* (Basil Davenport, ed.).

Carpe diem!

Music: Selected arias and overtures from Puccini and Verdi
Art: Cave paintings from France (and elsewhere)

---

## WEEK 15
**Boethius:** *The Consolations of Philosophy*

Boethius — "the most learned man of his day" — wrote this while in prison awaiting execution by the Ostrogoth King Theodoric. Philosophy appears in these pages as goddess and muse. The Empire was in decline; philosophy still offered wisdom and consolation. Timeless guidance for tough times.

Music: Schubert: String Quartet No. 14 (*Death and the Maiden*); Mozart: *Requiem*
Art: Albrecht Dürer; Hieronymus Bosch; Pieter Bruegel the Elder

## WEEK 16
**Lao Tzu:** *Tao Te Ching*
**Sun Tzu:** *The Art of War*

Two works of advice with entirely divergent ethos: one almost brutally practical, the other embracing multilayered and mystical thinking almost to the point of paradox. Reading both in the same week is almost a provocation.

Music: The Beatles (*White Album, Sgt. Pepper's, Abbey Road*); Rolling Stones (*Let It Bleed, Beggars Banquet, Exile on Main Street*)
Art: Chinese architecture

---

## WEEK 17
**Apuleius:** *The Golden Ass*

Rome in irreversible decline — but great works of art are sometimes born from disruption and decadence. *The Golden Ass* anticipates the rise of the novel and even postmodernism. Satirical, boundary-breaking, irreverent. Gives insights on the collapse of the classical world unavailable elsewhere.

Music: Scott Joplin: Rags and other piano works
Art: Vincent van Gogh

---

## WEEK 18
**Selections from** *The Arabian Nights*

Focus: the prologue (Scheherazade frame story) + five tales: "The Fisherman and the Jinni," "The Three Apples," "Sinbad the Sailor," "Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves," "Aladdin."

Music: Rimsky-Korsakov: *Scheherazade*; Tchaikovsky ballets: *Swan Lake, The Nutcracker, The Sleeping Beauty*
Art: Aboriginal Australian Art

---

## WEEK 19
***Bhagavad Gita***
***The Rule of St. Benedict***
**Augustine:** *The Confessions* Books 1–2

Cultivating the interior life. The Bhagavad Gita is ~160 pages (Penguin edition). The Rule of St. Benedict is short — a guide to monastic life. Begin Augustine's *Confessions*.

Music: Gregorian chants
Art: Giotto; Fra Angelico

---

## WEEK 20
**Augustine:** *Confessions* Books 2–13

The leading thinkers of a dying Roman empire are turning inward. Augustine of Hippo transforms Western narrative — his personal approach changes the whole tone. An important work in the history of memoir and autobiography, and a kind of self-help guide from a collapsing society.

Music: Bach: *St. Matthew Passion*
Art: The Bayeux Tapestry

---

## WEEK 21
**Geoffrey Chaucer:** Selections from *The Canterbury Tales* (Prologue, Knight's Tale, Miller's Tale, Wife of Bath's Tale)
**Sir Thomas Malory:** *Le Morte D'Arthur* Books 13–15 (the Holy Grail quest)

The two greatest works in English from the pre-Shakespeare period. Both are collections of stories so individual sections stand alone within our page constraint.

Music: Beethoven: Late String Quartets
Art: Pre-Raphaelite painters

![[Attachments/gioia-holy-grail-tapestry.jpeg]]
*The Arming and Departure of the Knights, Holy Grail tapestries (Edward Burne-Jones)*

## WEEK 22
***The Mwindo Epic***
***Sundiata: An Epic of Old Mali***

The epic tradition in Africa retains its musical connections. When researchers documented *The Mwindo Epic*, three percussionists accompanied the bard — and one typically took over the bard's job. Literature originated in music; these works take us directly back to the source.

Music: Fela Kuti (*Zombie, Coffin for Head of State*)
Art: Traditional African art

---

## WEEK 23
**The Letters of Abelard and Heloise**
**Love lyrics of the Qiyan, Troubadours, Trobairitz, and Trouvères**

Abelard and Heloise — the most famous romantic couple in medieval culture. Secular lyrics from various cultures and classes, from slaves to nobility.

Music: Madrigals from Monteverdi (and other composers)
Art: Gothic cathedrals (Notre Dame, Chartres, Cologne)

---

## WEEK 24
**Dante:** *La Vita Nuova*
**Dante:** *Inferno*

Dante's personal account *La Vita Nuova*, then descent into Hell. Pay special attention to how the poet transforms his own life story — romantic youth and midlife crisis — into art and embodied metaphysics. A significant moment in the advance of literary culture and narrative.

Music: Minimalist music: Terry Riley (*In C*), Steve Reich (*Music for 18 Musicians*), Philip Glass (*Koyaanisqatsi*), Pauline Oliveros (*Deep Listening*)
Art: The Ghent Altarpiece

![[Attachments/gioia-dante-beatrice.jpeg]]
*Dante sees his beloved Beatrice by the Arno River (Henry Holiday, 1883)*

## WEEK 25
**Dante:** *Purgatorio*
**Dante:** *Paradiso*

Rise from Hell to Purgatory and Paradise. More than 700 years later, Dante's way of merging the personal, the spiritual, the imaginative, and the romantic into a single narrative is still bold and rule-breaking.

Music: Bach: Mass in B Minor
Art: Native American Art

---

## WEEK 26
**Shakespeare:** *Hamlet, King Lear, Macbeth*

![[Attachments/gioia-shakespeare-first-folio.png]]

Three tragedies to start — more Shakespeare to come. Compare Shakespearean characters and conflicts to TV and commercial films today. Consider the richness of inner lives of these characters: we haven't experienced that level of psychological depth anywhere in our readings up to this point, and will only rarely match it in coming months.

Film versions are easy to find online; seeing them on screen or onstage gives a fuller experience.

Music: Berlioz: *Symphonie Fantastique*; Duke Ellington & Billy Strayhorn: *Such Sweet Thunder*; Shostakovich: *Hamlet, King Lear* (film soundtracks)
Art: Titian
