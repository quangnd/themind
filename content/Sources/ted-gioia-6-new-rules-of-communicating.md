The era of teleprompters and talking points has come to an end.

Opening: Socrates at his trial (399 BC) told the jury he had no skill at making speeches — only the everyday language of common people. He'd never studied rhetoric. "Unless indeed they call him a clever speaker who speaks the truth." He received the death penalty.

![[Attachments/gioia-communication-socrates.png]]
*Socrates defends himself at his trial (painting by Louis Joseph Lebrun, 1867)*

Western culture was built on one-way communication: leaders speak, the rest listen. Socrates was the last major thinker to rely solely on conversation. After his death, his successors wrote books and gave lectures.

![[Attachments/gioia-communication-lincoln.png]]
*This is how leaders once spoke to the people — but it's now changing.*

---

## The Shift

"Scripted" is now an insult. Plainspoken dialogue is more trustworthy. This is part of the up-versus-down revolution — a conflict that may have more impact on society than Left-versus-Right. The hierarchies we've inherited are toppling; in some cases reversing.

---

## The 6 New Rules

![[Attachments/gioia-6-rules-communication-chart.png]]

1. You gain more trust when seated, not standing.
2. Don't speak *at* people — speak *with* them.
3. An informal tone is more persuasive now. Even leaders must adjust.
4. Conversations have more influence than speeches.
5. Spontaneous communications from a personal standpoint are more "real" than scripted team-crafted messages.
6. Soundbites and talking points are less impactful than storytelling, humor, and off-the-cuff comments.

![[Attachments/gioia-communication-seated.png]]
*Leaders will now have more authority when they speak while sitting — not standing.*

## Why It's Happening

Rhetorical skills got degraded over the last decade. Top-down speechifying works when rigorous and logical — but the age of insults, taunts, and denunciations turned oratory into browbeating. Girardian mimicry: screaming from one elite causes others to scream back, escalating with each cycle. Most people are now burned out on hot oratory. Conversational style feels refreshing.

Gioia on his own writing: after launching on Substack, his sentences changed. He didn't understand why at first. "I only gradually figured out that I was now writing the same way I spoke in private conversation. It was almost as if I'd let my guard down, and was talking off the record, or with a close friend."

---

## The Historical Arc

- **1921:** Warren Harding hired the first presidential speechwriter. Woodrow Wilson was the last president to write his own speeches.
- **1940s–60s:** Walter Winchell — declamatory broadcaster, now sounds grating and fake.
- **1960s–70s:** Walter Cronkite — still staged and scripted; *The Tonight Show* (Johnny Carson) shows an alternative in the same era.
- **Now:** Joe Rogan's 3-hour podcasts — against all conventional wisdom about short soundbites — became the dominant format.

The old experts all agreed: nobody has time for a 3-hour podcast. They were wrong.

---

## What Comes Next

Institutional media will try to become edgy and freewheeling — "a dinosaur pretending that it's a ballerina." Those who cling to the bully pulpit will erode quickly. Politicians, broadcasters, educators, doctors, ministers, coaches, managers — anyone who exercises leadership in any group setting — must adapt.

"Oratory won't come back until genuine orators emerge as leaders."
