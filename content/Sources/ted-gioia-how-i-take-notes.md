# How I Take Notes

**Source:** The Honest Broker (2023-08-08)
**Author:** [[Ted Gioia]]
## Summary

Gioia shares his three-level framework for note-taking, developed over decades. The framework moves progressively from passive to active engagement: marking the book, summarizing it, then writing your own ideas sparked by it. The highest level is "taking notice" — using reading as a catalyst for original thought.

The opening story sets the frame: at 18, Gioia won a wine-tasting contest using only another person's richly descriptive tasting notes — wines he had never tasted. This revealed note-taking as a reconstruction and transmission tool, not just a memory aid.

## The Three Levels

### Level 1: Marking Up the Book

Underline key passages, add marginal comments, insert post-its, fold in newspaper reviews. This makes reading an active process — constantly searching for connections, surprising statements, larger patterns.

The greater benefit comes later: returning to a marked book months or years later allows re-experiencing all initial impressions without re-reading in full.

### Level 2: Summarizing the Book

After finishing, resist the temptation to immediately start the next book. Spend a couple more hours writing a brief holistic summary. This forces mental processing of what was learned and serves as a reference for revisiting the subject later.

### Level 3: Writing Down Your Own Ideas Spurred by the Book

The highest level — and, for Gioia, the most powerful. Not notes *about* the book but notes *generated by* it. This is "taking notice," not just "taking notes."

- Expands thinking by leaps and bounds
- Often serves as building blocks for articles and books
- Valuable even for non-writers: the greatest reward from reading is how it broadens you as the reader

Gioia didn't begin doing this consistently until approaching middle age, and considers it the most important form of note-taking.

His 400+ literary essays in *The Vault* (Conceptual Fiction section of The Honest Broker) began as this kind of extended reading note. Never commissioned, never requested — written for his own benefit and later published.

## Technique: Coherent Sentences Over Bullet Points

After the wine-tasting revelation, Gioia changed how he took notes entirely. He taught himself to write in complete sentences and integrated paragraphs in real time — even during live lectures.

This forced him to:
- Listen for connections and coherence, not just facts
- Improve his thinking and writing simultaneously
- Produce something holistic that could be understood months or years later

## Notes Compound Over Decades

Two key observations Gioia offers:

1. Notes that seemed useless at the time often proved valuable 10–20 years later. "We seldom understand the full benefit of high-level reading until long after the fact."
2. Notes from his 40s and 50s are far more useful than those from his 20s and 30s — depth of engagement increases over time.

## Three Reasons This Applies to Everyone

Gioia addresses the objection that this approach is only for writers:

1. **In any vocation** — he applied this to business reading (*Far Eastern Economic Review*, *HBR*, *Economist*, *McKinsey Quarterly*) during his non-writing career years. There is no career where tracking your learning isn't useful.
2. **Future value is unknowable** — you cannot know at the time which notes will prove valuable. Never assume a book has no future relevance.
3. **Discipline and mental training** — even summarizing a bad TV show or second-rate movie has value. The cognitive habit matters, independent of the subject.

## Key Quote

> The discipline of taking notes can evolve into taking notice — which is a much higher level activity.
