Or why it's 1800 all over again.

Started as a flippant remark — "technocracy had grown so oppressive and manipulative it would spur a backlash, and our rebellion might resemble the Romanticist movement of the early 1800s." Gioia took it seriously, conducted a deep dive into the history of Romanticism, and shares year-by-year research notes from 1800–1804.

Core claim: music is an early indicator of social change. The parallels between the turn of the 19th century and our own moment are not hard to see.

---

## The Parallel: circa 1800 and circa now

- Rationalist and algorithmic models were dominating every sphere of life — people started resisting
- Companies grew more powerful, promising productivity and prosperity. Blake called them "dark Satanic mills"; Luddites burned factories — the equivalent of throwing away your smartphone
- Even as science and technology produced amazing results, dysfunctional behaviors sprang up everywhere (Goethe's *Werther*, the Marquis de Sade, Gothic novels)
- The creative class increasingly attacked rationalist currents that had morphed into violent, intrusive forces — for Blake, the name Newton became a term of abuse
- Artists, especially poets and musicians, took the lead in the revolt, celebrating human feeling as more trustworthy and desirable than technology, profits, and cold calculation

> "Imagine a growing sense that algorithmic and mechanistic thinking has become too oppressive. Imagine if people started resisting technology. Imagine a revolt against STEM's dominance. Imagine people deciding that the good life starts with NOT learning how to code."

## Year-by-Year Notes: 1800–1804

### 1800

**Wordsworth's Lyrical Ballads manifesto:** Mocks previous writers for elitism. Insists the language of the common people is more artistic. Poets should focus on "incidents and situations from common life... in a selection of language really used by men." A missile aimed at Oxford and Cambridge. From this moment forward, hand-wringing over populist aesthetics will be the crisis that never ends in the Western world.

**Haydn's The Creation:** Published 1800. Haydn leaves church patronage behind — performs for paying ticket holders at Covent Garden. "Yes, we still praise the glory of God's creation, but from now on worshipful audiences will pay tithes to the composer."

**Beethoven at 30:** Over the next three years, Beethoven invents an entirely new emotional vocabulary for Western music. The B-flat Major Sonata, Op. 22 and especially the Sonata in A-flat Major, Op. 26 — the only Beethoven piano work Chopin performed regularly. "The seeds of all future Romanticist piano styles, from classical to cocktail, are sown here." Even sonata form gets thrown out the window.

**Schelling's *System of Transcendental Idealism*:** Art heals the divide between objectivity and subjectivity, inner life and outer world, philosophy and science. The unconscious is not something to fear — it is the source of freedom. "The Enlightenment is officially over."

### 1801

**Novalis dies:** After publishing his *Hymns to the Night*, which plays a key role in creating the noir aesthetic. "The first step in countering rationalism is to remove the light from the Enlightenment." An avalanche of poems about sleep, dreams, nightingales, stars, and the dark recesses of the inner life.

![[Attachments/gioia-caspar-david-friedrich.png]]
*Removing light from the Enlightenment — paintings of Caspar David Friedrich (1774–1840)*

**Gioia's hypothesis:** We are moving right now from an aesthetics of light to an aesthetics of dark. When rationalistic and algorithmic tyranny grows too extreme, art returns to the darkness of the unconscious life.

### 1802

**Beethoven at 31:** Contemplating suicide due to rapid-onset deafness. Retreats to Heiligenstadt. "I must live almost alone, like one who has been banished." His music grows more imperious and insistent. Breaks new ground constantly. "I still find it hard to grasp how his music advanced more radically after the onset of deafness. There must be a lesson in that."

![[Attachments/gioia-beethoven-at-30.webp]]
*Beethoven at age 30*

**Haydn:** Now referring to himself as "a priest of this sacred art." Great composers are no longer servants — they receive more acclaim than scientists or captains of industry.

### 1803

Haydn too ill to compose. Beethoven works on the *Eroica*. Nobody in the world has even started to assimilate what Beethoven is doing.

### 1804

**"Art for art's sake"** — first use of phrase dates from this year. More than a defense of creative spirit: "a rebuttal to scientism and its pretensions as the ultimate ruler of human destiny."

**Beethoven turns against Napoleon** — emblematic of the aesthetic reversal sweeping Europe. "It's somehow fitting that music takes the lead role in deconstructing a tyrannical rationalism, and proposing a more human alternative."
