# Politics and the English Language

George Orwell, 1946

Orwell's landmark essay arguing that bad writing and bad thinking are mutually reinforcing — and that the process is reversible. The essay diagnoses five categories of linguistic decay (dying metaphors, verbal false limbs, pretentious diction, meaningless words, and ready-made phrases), demonstrates the damage through five real specimens and one devastating parody (Ecclesiastes rewritten in modern bureaucratic English), and concludes with six rules for clear writing.

The political argument: when language becomes vague and dishonest, it enables political dishonesty. When political dishonesty demands euphemism, it degrades language further. The cycle can only be broken by individual writers making conscious choices at the sentence level.
