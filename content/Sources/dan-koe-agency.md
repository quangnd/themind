# The Most Important Skill in the Age of AI (Agency)

**Author:** Dan Koe (@thedankoe)
**Source:** The Dan Koe Letter (newsletter)

## Summary

Agency — the ability to iterate without permission — is the only skill that remains relevant regardless of what AI does to the job market. This article defines agency in depth, explains why most people lack it (learned helplessness, conformist development, specialist conditioning), argues why generalists win, and offers a practical protocol for developing agency through experimentation and iteration.

Structured in five sections:
1. **Agency is the ability to iterate without permission** — three traits: iterate without permission, treat life as an experiment, believe in the difficult; Seligman's learned helplessness
2. **AI is not a threat to the high-agency** — creators are context creators, not content creators; AI exposes what mattered all along
3. **Why generalists win** — Prussian education model; Shakespeare as synthesizer; tools evolve, vision doesn't
4. **The 5 human capabilities** — Computation, Transformation, Variation, Selection, Attention; why AGI doesn't make humans irrelevant
5. **How to practice agency** — research → experiment → identify patterns → create own process → pass it down; games as recorded agencies

## Key Claims

- ~50% of the population is at the conformist stage of development and lacks the cognitive capacity for genuine agency
- Agency is not just action but an undying commitment to iteration — learning and doing in unison
- Seligman's learned helplessness: people were trained to believe difficult goals are impossible, so they don't even try
- Creators are context creators, not content creators; content is meaningless without context
- The Prussian education model was designed to create compliant workers, not independent thinkers
- Specialists are attached to a skill; skills evolve and get replaced; generalists focus on the goal
- The 5 human capabilities (computation, transformation, variation, selection, attention) are unbounded — AGI shares the same physics constraints
- You develop agency by studying others' agencies, experimenting, identifying patterns, and creating your own process
