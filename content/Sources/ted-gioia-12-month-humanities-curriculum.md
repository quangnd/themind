Can you really learn this stuff by just reading 250 pages per week for a year? Let's try and find out.

Ever since I described my lifetime reading plan, people have asked me to recommend lists of books. A New York editor at a major imprint asked me to turn my article into a full-length guide to arts and culture. And two different 'great books' colleges inquired about my availability.

I've declined them — pursuing an education of this sort is a huge task, devised over the course of 50 years. It's a way of life, not a quick fix.

But people are hungry for an entry path into this stuff—and they deserve guidance and support. So I gave myself a challenge: what would I do if I had students for one year, willing to commit to reading around 250 pages per week?

![[Attachments/gioia-humanities-where-to-begin.jpeg]]

## Ground Rules

1. Around 250 pages of reading per week — no War and Peace
2. Music suggestion for each week
3. Visual arts suggestion for each week
4. Don't be discouraged by rough patches — "No pain, no gain"
5. Group reading multiplies the benefit, but solitary travelers also benefit

---

## WEEK ONE
**Plato:** *The Final Days of Socrates* (Euthyphro, Apology, Crito, Phaedo) + Republic Books I, VII

The most brilliant thinker of antiquity is forced to drink poison because the rulers didn't like what he was saying. Socrates defends himself, but ultimately loses his life because he pursues truth not power. Around 150 pages.

Republic Books I and VII add justice and the Allegory of the Cave. Once you know this cave, you see it everywhere.

Music: Bach: *The Well-Tempered Clavier* (Book 1) and *Goldberg Variations*
Art: Jacques-Louis David paintings, including *The Death of Socrates* (1787)

![[Attachments/gioia-death-of-socrates.jpeg]]

---

## WEEK TWO
**Sappho (and others):** Lyrics
**Homer:** *Odyssey* Books 1–10

The single most important moment in literary and musical history happened when people started singing about themselves and their feelings. Sappho did more than anyone to make this happen. The word *lyric* comes from the lyre — they were singer-songwriters.

Lattimore collection of Greek lyric poetry: ~100 pages, leaving room to start the Odyssey. Emily Wilson, Robert Fagles, or Robert Fitzgerald translations all work.

Music: Schubert's *Winterreise*; Joni Mitchell's *Blue*
Art: Ancient Greek sculpture

---

## WEEK THREE
**Homer:** *Odyssey* Books 11–24

The *Odyssey* is the first major book in the Western world that celebrates a hero for wisdom and intelligence, not just bravery. That's a turning point in cultural history. Brute force is no longer the highest ideal.

Odysseus must first visit the Underworld before finally returning to Ithaca — Gioia's favorite moment in all of literature.

Music: Beethoven's 3rd (Eroica), 5th, and 9th symphonies
Art: Ancient Greek architecture

---

## WEEK FOUR
**Confucius:** *The Analects*

A foundational text in world culture, studied for more than 2,000 years. Simultaneously a guide to personal conduct, a leadership book, a political philosophy, an educational program, and an introduction to Chinese culture. Arthur Waley's translation (including introduction): ~250 pages.

Music: *The Hugo Masters* — 4-album set of Chinese traditional music (Celestial Harmonies)
Art: Ancient Chinese arts and crafts

---

## WEEK FIVE
**Aristotle:** *Nicomachean Ethics* + *Poetics*

Aristotle's guide to virtue ethics, recommended as a superior alternative to utilitarian approaches like effective altruism. For living a holistic, integrated life, this is essential. *Poetics* is shorter — the most influential work in the history of cultural criticism.

Music: Bach: *Cello Suites*
Art: Gian Lorenzo Bernini sculpture and architecture

---

## WEEK SIX
**Plato:** *Symposium*
**Herodotus:** *The Histories* Books 1, 6–8

Plato's *Symposium* remains the most significant philosophical text on love. Herodotus provides a colorful, gossipy account of the Greco-Persian Wars — the greatest military and cultural conflict of antiquity.

Music: Nonesuch Explorer recordings — Japan, Tibet, Iran, Indonesia
Art: Persian art and architecture

---

## WEEK SEVEN
***The Epic of Gilgamesh***
***Dhammapada***

The *Epic of Gilgamesh* is a foundational Mesopotamian work unknown until the 19th century. It predates Homer and establishes epic literature as an older tradition than assumed. The story enriches notions of friendship, heroism, lamentation, and spirituality.

The *Dhammapada* — 423 verses from the Buddha — fills the remaining page count.

![[Attachments/gioia-gilgamesh-flood-tablet.jpeg]]

Music: Stravinsky: *The Rite of Spring*; Wagner overtures
Art: Ancient Mesopotamian art

---

## WEEK EIGHT
***Ancient Egyptian hymns, prayers and songs*** (Susan Tower Hollis collection)

Includes the *Egyptian Book of the Dead* and other religious/mythic texts, as well as the oldest secular love songs — predating even Sappho. Much of the value comes from navigating through cultures and worldviews different from our own.

Music: Oud music from Southern Egypt; Desert blues (Ali Farka Touré, Tinariwen); Youssou N'Dour's *Egypt*
Art: Ancient Egyptian architecture and art

---

## WEEK NINE
**Bible:** Genesis, Ecclesiastes, The Four Gospels, Epistle to the Romans

Foundational texts for multiple religions and any humanities education. These writings help navigate the daily news and social interactions — the context in which Western civilization operates.

Music: William Byrd's choral music; Thomas Tallis: *Lamentations of Jeremiah*; Handel's *Messiah*
Art: Michelangelo: Sistine Chapel and sculptures

---

## WEEK TEN
**Sophocles:** *Oedipus the King, Oedipus at Colonus, Antigone*
**Aeschylus:** *Agamemnon*
**Euripides:** *The Bacchae*
**Aristophanes:** *Lysistrata*

Comedy and tragedy. All three Theban plays, plus one each of Aeschylus, Euripides, and Aristophanes — offering insight into institutions, belief systems, gender roles, rituals, and ecstatic practices of antiquity. The Aristotle *Poetics* from Week 5 prepared us for this.

Music: Early blues (Blind Lemon Jefferson, Blind Willie Johnson, Son House, Robert Johnson, Bessie Smith)
Art: Ancient Greek pottery

---

## WEEK ELEVEN
**Marcus Aurelius:** *Meditations*
**Epictetus:** *The Enchiridion*

Stoicism was the most persuasive alternative to Christianity in Imperial Rome — both offered practical guidance and deeper philosophical currents. Marcus Aurelius ruled as Roman Emperor; Epictetus was born a slave. Both found the Stoic framework robust and flexible enough for their circumstances.

Music: Haydn Symphonies 45 (Farewell), 94 (Surprise), 104 (London)
Art: Ancient Roman art and architecture

---

## WEEK TWELVE
**Suetonius:** *The Twelve Caesars*

This week is devoted to decadence. Read about the twelve Caesars — but don't try to emulate them.

![[Attachments/gioia-twelve-caesars.jpeg]]

Music: Mozart: Symphonies 39–41
Art: Botticelli and Caravaggio

---

## WEEK THIRTEEN
***Koran:*** Surahs 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 12, 17, 18, 32, 36, 55, 67, 103, 112
**Rumi:** Selected Poems

Fourteen surahs from the Koran, supplemented with poems from Rumi.

Music: Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan; Religious Songs and Cantillations from Tunisia (Folkways)
Art: Islamic architecture
