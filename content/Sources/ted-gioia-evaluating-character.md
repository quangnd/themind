# My 8 Best Techniques for Evaluating Character

**Source:** The Honest Broker (2023-01-19)
**Author:** [[Ted Gioia]]

## Summary

Gioia offers 8 practical techniques for reading people, developed after paying a high price early in life for trusting what people said rather than what they revealed through behavior. The unifying principle: **observe what people do, not what they say** — and look for moments when the social performance drops away.

## The 8 Techniques

**1. Forget what they say — look at who they marry**
A person's choice of closest lifelong partner is more revealing than anything they say or do in public. It tells you about their innermost longings, what they think they deserve, and their deepest priorities and values.

**2. See how they treat service workers**
People reveal their true natures when dealing with those who have no power and can never return a favor. The social performance drops — the true self comes forward. Abuse of service workers is a reliable indicator of untrustworthiness; extraordinary care is one of the strongest indicators of trustworthiness.

**3. Discover what experiences formed their character in early life**
Character and the ability to handle challenges are almost entirely formed during the first two decades of life. Gioia encountered a CEO who spent entire interviews solely asking about childhood and teenage years — and had an impressive track record of correctly assessing people. The distance someone traveled *before* formal credentials is often the most significant data.

**4. How do they invest their two most valuable resources?**
Calendar and budget reveal what a person is really about — far more than their stated values. Time and money are precious; words are cheap. Watch how people allocate both.

**5. Identify what irritates people most in others**
The flaw people hate most in others is usually their own greatest weakness. When we see a weakness in someone else, they function as a mirror — and we dislike seeing our own flaws reflected. The things that make someone lose all tolerance are usually the things they struggle with daily in themselves.

**6. Can they listen?**
The rarest skill. Great listeners are among the most effective people Gioia has encountered — they can assess situations with accuracy and act to maximize group effectiveness. Most people either talk or do neither. The small number who genuinely listen are invaluable.

**7. If they cheat at small things, they will cheat at big things**
A man who moves his golf ball when no one is watching will break larger rules when higher stakes are involved. The scale changes; the character doesn't. Don't dismiss small dishonesty as trivial — it is the most reliable preview of large dishonesty.

**8. Watch how they handle unexpected problems**
You can't tell how a person improvises until they face something unscripted. Some companies stage mishaps during senior hiring interviews for exactly this reason. Over-reaction and under-reaction are both failures. Those who rise to the occasion — calm, decisive, neither explosive nor passive — reveal something essential about their core values.

## Closing Note

Gioia adds: these techniques can be turned inward. The same methods for evaluating others apply to self-evaluation. "Perhaps the character you need to assess is your own."
