# Cybernetics

From the Greek *kybernetikos* — "to steer" or "good at steering." Cybernetics is the science of goal-directed, self-correcting systems. In the context of personal development, it is the framework for understanding what intelligence actually is and how to develop it.

Naval Ravikant's definition: **"The only real test of intelligence is if you get what you want out of life."** Cybernetics is the mechanism that determines whether you can.

---

## The Properties of Intelligent Systems

Cybernetics maps the loop that all intelligent systems run, biological or mechanical:

1. **Have a goal** — a desired state
2. **Act** toward that goal
3. **Sense** where you currently are
4. **Compare** current position to the goal
5. **Act again** based on that feedback

A ship blown off course that corrects toward its destination. A thermostat sensing temperature change and responding. A pancreas adjusting insulin after a blood glucose spike. All are running this loop.

Intelligence, in this framework, is measured by the system's **ability to iterate and persist with trial and error**. Not raw IQ. Not knowledge. The capacity to:
- Take feedback honestly
- Adjust behavior accordingly
- Repeat until the gap between current state and goal closes

---

## High vs. Low Intelligence

**High intelligence** (in this sense): the ability to iterate, persist, and understand the big picture. Any problem can be solved on a long enough timescale. There is always a series of choices that leads to the goal — even if the exact path requires resources or knowledge not yet available.

**Low intelligence**: the inability to learn from mistakes. Getting stuck on a problem rather than solving it. Hitting a roadblock and quitting. The writer who can't build a readership and concludes they're simply not good enough, rather than experimenting until they find what works.

This is not about raw cognitive ability. It is about the relationship between action and feedback. A "low-intelligence" move (in this framework) is treating failure as evidence that the goal is impossible, rather than as data about what to try next.

## Goals as the Operating System

Cybernetics places **goals at the center of everything**. They are not just targets — they are the operating system through which reality is perceived. A goal determines:
- What information you notice (the [[Reticular Activating System]] filters for it)
- What counts as progress or failure
- What actions feel natural or forced

For most people, their goals were not consciously chosen. They were conditioned — programmed by parents, schools, and culture into the psyche before critical thinking was available. "Go to school, get the job, get offended, play victim, retire at 65." A known path running on autopilot.

To become more intelligent (in this sense), you must:
- Reject the conditioned known path
- Dive into the unknown
- Set new, higher goals that expand what you perceive
- Embrace chaos as the disorientation of growth
- Study generalized principles rather than domain-specific tricks
- Become a deep generalist

This sequence produces an extraordinary density of novel connections — which is what we observe, from the outside, as "an intelligent person."

---

## The Connection to Identity

Cybernetics and [[Identity-First Change]] are the same framework from different angles. Identity is a set of goals running unconsciously — "I am the type of person who..." is a goal-projection that filters perception and conditions behavior. Change the goals, and the identity follows. The [[Ego Development Stages]] map, in cybernetic terms, is a progression of increasingly sophisticated goal-structures: Stage 3 goals are conformist survival goals; Stage 7 goals can hold system-level complexity and self-correct at the meta level.
