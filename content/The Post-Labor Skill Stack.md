# The Post-Labor Skill Stack

A skill hierarchy from [[Dan Koe]] for what actually matters as AI automates the labor economy. Deliberately not domain-specific — the point is that career-specific skills (writing, coding, design) are secondary to the human capacities that make those skills worth paying for.

The stack is ordered: the higher skills create the conditions that make the lower ones effective. Mastering persuasion without agency produces a sophisticated operator who builds someone else's thing. Developing technical know-how without taste produces efficient content that says nothing.

---

## 1. Agency — The Meta-Skill

> *"The ability to act without permission or outside prompting."*

Agency is the capacity to author your own story — to set a trajectory and make the billion small and large decisions that move you forward, backward, and sideways. Within those decisions lies the raw material of everything you will ever create.

In the premodern worldview, agency could get you killed. In the modern worldview, it was partially allowed within institutional structures. In the intelligence age, **agency is the survival skill** — no institution will supply your identity, direction, or meaning. You have to create it.

Agency is developed through the three generators of meaning (see [[The Meaning Economy]]):

- **Struggle** — Deliberate choices, rejection of conformity, stepping into the arena, creating the potential for failure, developing a character arc. The default path society assigns is the path of maximum replaceability.
- **Status** — In the post-labor world, money shifts from a productivity metric to a tool to express agency. More options → more unique decision-making → more complex story.
- **Curiosity** — How you, specifically, filter signal from noise. Distraction and numbness erode curiosity. Curiosity is how you notice the path that only you could take.

Conforming to the idea that you "should have agency" is a low-agency behavior. Genuine agency is demonstrated through action that diverges from what would have been expected.

See [[Agency]] for the full development of this concept — including the three traits, learned helplessness, and how to practice it.

## 2. Taste — The Skill of Discernment

> *"Curation is a far more important skill than creation."*

AI creates what Koe calls the **infinite library problem**: if a library contained every possible arrangement of characters, it would contain all truths, all falsehoods, and all gibberish — and would be effectively useless. Finding meaningful signal would be statistically impossible. AI produces infinite content; taste is what makes any of it matter.

It also creates the **infinite monkey problem**: infinite output without intention or selection behind it is meaningless regardless of local quality.

Taste is the ability to discern what's worth keeping and what isn't. But: if you don't have full control over the selection process, you're being molded by the taste of another. Developing taste requires **building your own thing** — because you can only genuinely curate what you have taken full responsibility for.

Taste is also the antidote to postmodern aperspectivalism (see [[Techno-Economic Evolution]]). Developing taste requires making the commitment that *some things are better than others* — a stance postmodern culture discourages. This is where the next skill becomes necessary.

## 3. Perspective — Expanding the Mind's Capacity

> *"Your individual perspective may be the one thing that AGI can't replace."*

Developmental psychology documents that human values and worldviews evolve through predictable stages (see [[Ego Development Stages]] for the full 9-stage map) — from highly conformist and ideological to increasingly complex, nuanced, and self-authored. This is not a moral judgment but a cognitive one: higher stages can hold more contradiction without defaulting to defensive rejection.

Koe estimates ~50% of people operate at a highly conformist developmental stage — meaning most people lack the cognitive architecture for genuine agency. They hear that agency is good and then *conform to that idea*, which is the opposite of agency.

What actually expands perspective:

- **Allow disorientation.** When a new job, relationship, or culture knocks you out of equilibrium, resist the urge to immediately re-stabilize by doubling down on your prior frame. The dissonance is the growth mechanism.
- **Sit with complexity longer.** The instinct to resolve ambiguity quickly — take the political opinion, adopt the ideology, close the loop — is exactly what prevents perspective from deepening.
- **Avoid the false transformation.** Using a higher-stage vocabulary (spirituality, systems thinking, integral theory) as a status signal while still behaving at a lower stage. This is common and blocks real development.

Greater perspective is what makes taste more refined and agency more coherent. It is also, per [[The Swap Test]], the most AI-resistant property: AI cannot access your evolving perspective — only simulate where it was.

## 4. Persuasion — Getting People to Care

> *"Art must merge with business."*

Having created something meaningful is not sufficient. The history of art is full of genuinely great work that no one ever saw. Persuasion is the skill of getting people to care — not manipulation, but understanding how attention and value signal reach other people.

When work existed in villages and tribes, distribution happened automatically. You made something, the community needed it, exchange happened. In the digital world, you are seemingly alone in a vast space. Distribution requires active effort.

The attention mechanisms (marketing, sales, social media, copywriting) are lower in the stack because they are most effectively learned by building your own thing, making mistakes, and correcting them. They cannot be effectively applied without the higher-order skills — persuasion without taste produces slick emptiness; persuasion without perspective produces sophisticated conformity.

---

## 5. Technical Know-How — Utilizing AI and Tools

The bottom of the stack — not because it doesn't matter, but because it is the most domain-specific, the most rapidly evolving, and the most amenable to learning-on-the-job. Everyone will work with AI in some capacity; the question is what you direct it toward.

The practical approach: experiment. Try AI on your next project. Ask it to identify which tasks don't require a personal touch and hand those off. Use it for cognitive offload so the higher-order skills — agency, taste, perspective — have more bandwidth. Technical know-how is what amplifies the stack; it doesn't replace it.

---

## The Stack as a System

The skills compound upward: technical know-how without perspective produces more of what already exists. Perspective without persuasion never reaches anyone. Persuasion without taste produces noise at scale. Taste without agency produces sophisticated derivative work. Agency running all the way through is what produces a creative whose work is genuinely irreplaceable — work that passes [[The Swap Test]] in the other direction.
