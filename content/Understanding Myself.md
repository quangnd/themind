## How to Improve

### 1. The "knowing-doing gap" is my central challenge

I already _know_ most of what I need. My vault is full of excellent principles. The issue isn't knowledge — it's **execution and consistency**. This is common for people with high curiosity and multiple interests.

> Instead of learning new methods, spend the next 3 months **only executing** what I already know. The [[90-Day English Speaking Fluency Plan]] is a perfect test of this.

### 2. Use writing as my mirror

I wrote: "Viết kiểm tra những gì ta nghĩ là mình biết" — writing tests what we think we know. My daily writing practice section is the most powerful self-understanding tool I already have.

> In my daily note's "Writing practice" section, once a week write about **myself** — not about what I learned, but about what I noticed about my own behavior. "Today in the meeting I abandoned my script again. Why? Was I nervous or overconfident?"

### 3. Track patterns, not just tasks

My weaknesses list is static. I wrote it once and it sits there.

> Create a simple weekly reflection with just 3 questions:
>
> - What did I start but not finish this week?
> - When did I react emotionally instead of calmly?
> - What did I do that my future self will thank me for?

### 4. Go deep on fewer things

I wrote: "Đào nhiều hố hay đào một cái hố thật sâu?" and concluded I need **deep holes, not many shallow ones.** But my behavior still leans toward many shallow holes.

> Apply a "3 things only" rule each quarter. This quarter (Q1-Q2 2026):
>
> 1. English speaking fluency (90-day plan)
> 2. AI business research
> 3. Financial discipline
>
> Say **no** to everything else. When tempted by a new topic, write it in Inbox and come back in July.

### 5. Use my mental models on myself

I love IFS ([[Inversion]], [[First principles]], [[Second-order thinking]]). Turn them inward:

- **Inversion:** "What would make me fail at the 90-day English plan?" → Avoid those things.
- **First Principles:** "What's the minimum daily action that guarantees progress?" → The 30-min low-energy routine.
- **Second-order thinking:** "If I chase this new shiny thing now, what's the cost in 3 months?" → Lost momentum on what matters.
