# Metacrisis

The Metacrisis is a framework developed by [[Daniel Schmachtenberger]] describing the convergence of interconnected civilizational threats. It's not any single crisis (climate, AI, governance) but the underlying *pattern* that generates all of them — hence "meta."

## The Three Generator Functions

Schmachtenberger identifies three forces (generator functions) that, when they converge, drive civilizations toward catastrophe:

### 1. Rivalrous Dynamics
Win-lose games where one party's gain requires another's loss. Examples:
- Arms races between nations
- Corporate competition that externalizes costs
- Social media creators abandoning truth for engagement
- Academic publishing that hoards data to publish first

In the context of the [[Epistemic Commons]], rivalrous dynamics mean creators optimize for reaction rather than transformation, because the algorithm rewards engagement.

### 2. Substrate Consumption
"Substrate" is what something needs to exist — soil for plants, attention for media, trust for markets. When systems consume their foundation faster than it regenerates, collapse becomes inevitable. Examples:
- Depleting topsoil that took millennia to form
- **The attention economy consuming human cognitive capacity faster than it recovers**

This is the most insidious of the three when applied to the information environment. The very capacity humans need to *solve* civilizational problems is being consumed by the systems creating those problems.

### 3. Exponential Technology
Tools and systems that improve themselves at accelerating rates, outpacing human wisdom. Examples:
- AI doubling in capability faster than we can develop alignment
- Automated weapons systems
- Social media algorithms evolving faster than we can study their psychological effects

AI doesn't just create more content — it accelerates *imitation*. When the dominant content is destructive, AI scales destruction at unprecedented speed.

## The Three Attractors

When these forces converge, complex societies tend to fall into one of three "basins" (attractors):

1. **Civilizational collapse** — nuclear war, unaligned AI, ecological destruction, engineered pandemics
2. **Dystopian control** — total surveillance, digital authoritarianism, elimination of individual agency
3. **The third attractor** — a world where sense-making, shared understanding, and aligned incentives exist

The third attractor is the only desirable outcome, and reaching it requires a healthy [[Epistemic Commons]] and a population capable of genuine thinking.

## Connection to Content and Essays

Koe applies Schmachtenberger's framework specifically to the internet and content creation:

- **Rivalrous dynamics** → creators compete for attention, abandoning truth
- **Substrate consumption** → cognitive capacity atrophies as the attention economy accelerates
- **Exponential technology** → AI floods the commons with imitation thinking

The antidote, Koe argues, is [[Fast Content vs Slow Content|slow content]] — particularly [[Essays as Thinking Tools|essays]] — that requires genuine cognitive effort from both creator and consumer.
