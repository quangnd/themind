# Clarity is Overrated

[[Ted Gioia]]'s argument that the modern obsession with clarity — carried to its extreme in AI-generated writing — strips language of the very qualities that make it human. Ambiguity isn't a bug; it's the feature that forces deeper thinking, invites interpretation, and preserves the mystery that is essential to being alive.

---

## The Argument

Gioia draws on his own biography: trained first at Oxford in analytic philosophy (where mathematical precision was the only acceptable mode), then at Stanford's Graduate School of Business (where even words were suspect — numbers were better). He eventually became a master of the three-paragraph memo. And he hated it.

> "Writing should open up the horizon, not hide it from view."

The clarity cult treats language as a delivery mechanism for pre-formed conclusions. But that's not what language does at its best — it opens a conversation, not closes one.

---

## The Tradition of Productive Ambiguity

Ambiguity is not a modern deficiency to be corrected. It is a deliberate technique with a long and honored lineage:

- **Taoism and Zen** — wisdom transmitted through paradox and koan, not proposition
- **The Oracle at Delphi** — guidance arrived as riddle, not instruction
- **Shakespeare** — the Fool speaks truth; meaning lives in the indirection
- **Blake and Wordsworth** — the child sees what the educated adult cannot
- **Dostoevsky's *The Idiot*** — the "idiot" is the one who sees clearly; his "nonsense" (non-sense) is the spiritual pathway
- **Kierkegaard** — laughed at anyone who would give three arguments for falling in love; love exists on a higher plane than argumentation
- **Plato's dialectic** — the open-ended Socratic question is not a failure to arrive at clarity; it *is* the pedagogy

The continental philosophers dismissed at Oxford — Sartre, Nietzsche, Heidegger, Kierkegaard, Schopenhauer, Bergson, Foucault, Husserl — were not unclear because they were bad writers. They were writing about things that resist final formulation. Their difficulty was proportional to the depth of their subject matter.

---

## AI Writing as the Endpoint of the Clarity Cult

The rise of AI-generated writing brings this argument into sharp relief. AI is the purest expression of the clarity ideal: every sentence crisp, every claim stated, nothing left open. And the result is sterility.

Gioia's diagnosis is precise:
- AI can *pretend* to experience human touchstones — love, faith, hope, grief — but cannot actually experience them
- This is *mauvaise foi* (Sartre's "bad faith"): lying to oneself, or worse, not having a self to lie to
- The sterile AI style is now the **dominant communication style** of our time — and increasingly the dominant *thinking* style

The Silicon Valley CEO personality is the human version of this: analytically sharp, emotionally cold, unable to read the room even when they own the building.

> "This is the deadliest myth of our time — the myth of some final clarity, preferably reduced to a chatbot response."

---

## What Gioia Wants Instead

Not the abolition of clarity — he is careful here. There is a place for it. But it belongs in a humble seat, not at the head of the table.

What belongs at the head of the table:
- **Emotional warmth**
- **Psychological depth**
- **Poetic feeling**
- **Human connection**
- **Fuzzier writing** — metaphors, sublime indirection, open-ended ambiguity that invites readers inside the text

The payoff is pedagogical: we think *better* when grappling with a metaphor than processing a series of logical propositions. Teachers will eventually rediscover this. Employers will hire for it. Audiences will seek it out.

## The Deeper Claim

Ambiguity is not a concession to imprecision. It is a different kind of precision — one calibrated to things that actually matter: love, faith, beauty, the depths of human experience. When you force those subjects into propositional clarity, you don't clarify them. You falsify them.

> "The inner stirrings of our soul cannot be framed with mathematical rigor."
